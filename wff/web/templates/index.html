{# --------------------------------------------------- what it's doing #}

What the machine actually does

the three steps, in plain words

Step 1 — find the faces. Every photo is opened at full resolution, turned the right way up, and searched for faces — both the whole frame and in overlapping tiles, so a small face in the back row isn’t missed. Faces too small, too blurry or too side-on are set aside. This is the slow part, and it’s saved to disk, so it only ever happens once per folder.

Step 2 — group them. Each face becomes 512 numbers. Faces with close numbers are piled together, tightly at first, then piles are merged only when there’s evidence. One rule is free and absolute: two faces in the same photo are never the same person. This part takes seconds, which is why you can re-tune it as often as you like.

Step 3 — you check it. The review page shows every pile in full. Marking a pile right or a face wrong isn’t just a note: it becomes the answer key, and every future run is scored against it.
